\section{Preliminary Evidence and Evaluation Plan}
\label{sec:evidence}

\paragraph{On-emulator attack reproduction.} On an Android emulator, an open notification-listener agent read a synthetic OTP from an SMS notification and forwarded it to a loopback endpoint. Across ten independent synthetic canaries, all ten were read and forwarded (observed exposure rate $1.0$ with a Wilson 95\% interval of $[0.72,1.0]$). This is a functional reproduction of the mechanism, not a device result and not a paper number.

\paragraph{Reference-model study (simulation only).} To check the qualitative trends the design predicts, we ran the project's Python reference model on synthetic canaries (seed \texttt{20261017}, $200$ trials per attack across eight attack families, $6400$ trials total), including read-and-exfiltrate (family~1) and identity ambiguity (family~7). The model is a simulation; its numbers are \emph{not} device measurements and are \emph{not} reported as results. Under it, the unprotected baseline exposed the value in every trial; a detected-placeholder baseline removed the literal value from the immediate artifact but still leaked it after entry (post-entry leakage $1.0$) and still allowed redirection ($1.0$), because it does not bind a destination; and the \SH{} condition produced zero exposure, zero redirection, zero post-entry leakage, zero forgery and replay acceptance, with full legitimate-task success and zero false rejections. On identity ambiguity, \SH{} failed closed (rejected $200/200$) while the baselines delivered the value ($200/200$). The platform-flow baseline was recorded as not applicable because it could not be exercised. Full configuration, outputs, and caveats are in the project's preliminary-evidence document.

\paragraph{Killer experiment (design of record).} The decisive experiment runs the OTP-forwarding injection against an open login-plus-OTP app, first unprotected (the agent forwards the code), then with \SH{}, where the OTP is a handle bound to the login app's package. It passes only if all three hold: (a) the raw code is absent from every instrumented agent artifact; (b) \texttt{ENTER(handle)} into the attacker's destination is rejected by the resolver; and (c) the legitimate login succeeds. In the reference model all three hold; establishing them on a real device is full-paper work.

\paragraph{Planned evaluation.} The full paper evaluates four conditions---unprotected, detected placeholder, platform flow, and \SH---against attack families: read-and-exfiltrate; destination redirection; fake handle; replay/race; post-entry reread; same-destination semantic misuse; and identity ambiguity. Metrics are exposure $E$, redirection $R$, utility $U$, post-entry leakage $L$, forgery acceptance $F$, and replay acceptance $Y$, with policy-rejection and false-rejection rates and latency. Binary outcomes use Wilson intervals; paired comparisons use a paired permutation or exact McNemar test; sample sizes are fixed by a prospective precision analysis. Exclusions (crash, outage, cancellation, unsupported identity) are reported by category. General-task regression is measured on the AndroidWorld suite \cite{rawles2024androidworld}, with SPA-Bench \cite{chen2024spabench} for additional breadth. Device latency, energy, and hardware-keystore behavior are explicitly deferred and reported only where a suitable device is available.
