\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\SH{} reframes how an untrusted mobile AI agent handles sensitive credentials: instead of exposing raw secrets to multi-modal observation and relying on fragile prompt adherence, the OS mints an opaque, unforgeable, destination- and task-bound handle and enforces zero-exposure resolution directly into verified UI input fields. Raw secrets never enter the agent's context, cannot be redirected by prompt injection, cannot be replayed, and remain shielded from visual observation.

Our extensive empirical evaluation on Android~14 across 10 benchmark applications and 6 research questions demonstrates that \SH{} completely closes the destination redirection vulnerability ($0/30$ leakage vs.\ $30/30$ on state-of-the-art baselines) with negligible microbenchmark overhead ($5.61\,\mu\text{s}$ mint, $0.13\,\mu\text{s}$ resolve) and zero benign task interference. By demonstrating that privacy and autonomous agent utility can coexist without mutual sacrifice, \SH{} establishes a principled foundation for OS-level secret mediation in next-generation mobile systems.
