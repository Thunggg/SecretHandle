\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Placeholder and anonymization defenses.} Mobile placeholder schemes replace sensitive rendered text with a type-preserving token and reverse the mapping at use time \cite{zhao2026anonymization}; the closest recent work resolves a \texttt{type(PLACEHOLDER)} locally but is detector-based, session-scoped, and validates index ranges rather than which field receives a resolved value. We differ on mint source (authoritative OS source, not screen scraping), unforgeability, destination-and-task binding, typed lifecycle, and a threat model that includes redirection.

\paragraph{Capability and isolation approaches.} Planner-side capability schemes and dual-model patterns hide values from the planner in tool-calling settings \cite{debenedetti2025camel,costa2025fides}; isolation and privilege-minimization designs restrict what a tool agent may do \cite{bagdasarian2024airgap,wu2025isolategpt,shi2025progent}. These operate in the planning or tool layer rather than at the point where a secret crosses from an OS source into a UI field.

\paragraph{Prompt sanitizers.} Detection-and-rewrite approaches suppress or restore sensitive spans in prompts \cite{siyan2025papillon,zeng2025privacyrestore,chong2024casper}. They reduce model-side exposure but do not bind a resolved value to a destination and are not OS-enforced.

\paragraph{Platform and industry mechanisms.} The SMS Retriever and User Consent APIs \cite{androidSmsRetriever,androidSmsConsent}, the sensitive-notification OTP pipeline \cite{android15privacy}, Credential Manager \cite{androidCredentialManager}, autofill \cite{androidAutofill,androidAutofillServices}, the clipboard sensitivity flag \cite{androidClipboard}, and Protected Confirmation \cite{androidProtectedConfirmation} are building blocks, but autofill and Credential Manager are app-initiated and trust the app, and server-side agentic-payment tokens are payment-only. \SH{} composes these and adds OS-level destination binding and lifecycle.

\paragraph{OS mechanisms and reference monitors.} \SH{} is an application of complete mediation and least privilege \cite{saltzer1975protection} to a new asset class: secrets that are typed into a UI by an untrusted process. Capability systems such as Capsicum bind authority to a handle and confine it to a scope \cite{watson2010capsicum}, and Android itself mediates permissions and app isolation through its install-time and runtime models \cite{felt2011permission,bugiel2013flexible,rossi2021seapp}. Taint and dynamic-analysis systems track the flow of sensitive data through applications \cite{enck2010taintdroid,yan2012droidscope}, which is complementary but observability-oriented: they can flag a leak, whereas \SH{} prevents the value from entering the agent's observation at all. The capability concept itself originates with Dennis and Van Horn \cite{dennis1966semantics}; our contribution is the destination-and-task predicate over package, origin, and field class together with an authenticated, lifecycle-bounded handle that an accessibility agent can invoke without seeing the value.

\paragraph{Credential and token standards.} OTP construction and validation are standardized by HOTP and TOTP \cite{rfc4226,rfc6238}, and token binding schemes constrain where an OAuth-bound token may be replayed \cite{rfc8707,rfc9449,rfc9700}. WebAuthn and passkeys remove a shared secret from the flow entirely \cite{webauthn3,nist80063b}. These authenticate a credential, a user, or a server endpoint, but they do not decide which on-device UI field may receive a captured value; \SH{} complements them by constraining the resolution sink inside the OS.

\paragraph{Attacks.} Documented agent OTP exfiltration and forwarding, plus environmental and fine-print injection, motivate the threat model \cite{du2025mobileattacks,liao2024eia,chen2025fineprint,greshake2023indirect,debenedetti2024agentdojo,zhan2024injecagent}. We adapt attack mechanics, not their reported success numbers.
